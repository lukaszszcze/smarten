Word formation and wisielec

Adding an ending

When an ending goes onto a word, the last letter of the word sometimes changes: a silent e drops or stays, a y becomes i, and -ic needs -ally. A few rules cover almost every word. (A letter that doubles, as in forgettable, is on the double letters page.)

Common mistakes

Common mistakeknowledgable
Rightknowledgeable

The e stays after g, so the g still sounds like j.

Common mistakeunrecogniseable
Rightunrecognisable

recognise loses its e before -able.

Common mistakeaccidentaly
Rightaccidentally

accidental + ly: keep both l's.

Common mistaketruely
Righttruly

true loses its e, although -ly starts with a consonant.

Common mistakearguement
Rightargument

argue loses its e, although -ment starts with a consonant.

The rule

The letters that go are crossed out and the letters that come in, or stay where you might drop them, are green. Harder words are in the "Advanced" box under each list.

1. A silent e at the end

The e drops before a vowel

  • adventure→adventurous
  • arrive→arrival
  • believe→believable
  • continue→continuous
  • create→creation, creative
  • educate→education
  • excite→exciting
  • fame→famous
  • globe→global
  • hope→hoping
  • ice→icy
  • imagine→imaginative
  • love→lovable
  • nature→natural
  • nerve→nervous
  • noise→noisy
  • please→pleasure
  • recognise→recognisable, unrecognisable
  • secure→security
  • sense→sensible
  • taste→tasty
  • use→usable
  • value→valuable
  • write→writing
Advanced: 6 more words
  • debate→debatable
  • desire→desirable
  • pure→purity
  • refuse→refusal
  • scarce→scarcity
  • stone→stony

The e stays before a consonant

  • achieve→achievement
  • advertise→advertisement
  • amuse→amusement
  • care→careful, careless
  • complete→completely
  • encourage→encouragement
  • entire→entirely
  • excite→excitement
  • extreme→extremely
  • grace→graceful
  • home→homeless
  • hope→hopeful, hopeless
  • immediate→immediately
  • lone→lonely
  • love→lovely
  • manage→management
  • measure→measurement
  • move→movement
  • nice→nicely
  • peace→peaceful
  • polite→politeness
  • rare→rarely
  • replace→replacement
  • rude→rudeness
  • safe→safely, safety
  • severe→severely
  • shame→shameful
  • sincere→sincerely
  • state→statement
  • use→useful, useless
  • waste→wasteful
Advanced: 2 more words
  • late→lateness
  • settle→settlement

But in these words the e drops before a consonant

  • argue→argument
  • awe→awful
  • due→duly
  • nine→ninth
  • true→truly, truth
  • whole→wholly
  • wise→wisdom

The e stays after c and g, so they stay soft

  • advantage→advantageous
  • change→changeable
  • courage→courageous
  • enforce→enforceable
  • knowledge→knowledgeable
  • manage→manageable
  • notice→noticeable
  • outrage→outrageous
  • replace→replaceable
  • service→serviceable
  • trace→traceable

The e stays after ee, oe and ye

  • agree→agreeable
  • canoe→canoeing
  • dye→dyeing (to colour something)
  • flee→fleeing
  • see→seeing

ie becomes y before -ing

  • die→dying
  • lie→lying
  • tie→tying

Drop the e before an ending that starts with a vowel (-able, -ing, -ous, -al, -ity, -ion, -y). Keep it before an ending that starts with a consonant (-ment, -ful, -less, -ly, -ness).

2. y becomes i

y becomes i

  • angry→angrily
  • apply→appliance
  • beauty→beautiful
  • busy→business
  • carry→carriage
  • cry→cried
  • deny→denial
  • easy→easily
  • empty→emptiness
  • envy→envious
  • friendly→friendliness
  • fury→furious
  • glory→glorious
  • happy→happiness, happily
  • heavy→heaviness
  • industry→industrial, industrious
  • lazy→laziness, lazily
  • lonely→loneliness
  • luxury→luxurious
  • marry→marriage
  • mystery→mysterious
  • pity→pitiful
  • plenty→plentiful
  • ready→readiness, readily
  • rely→reliable, reliance
  • silly→silliness
  • try→trial
  • ugly→ugliness
  • vary→various
  • victory→victorious
Advanced: 8 more words
  • ally→alliance
  • bury→burial
  • colony→colonial
  • comply→compliance
  • defy→defiance
  • harmony→harmonious
  • mercy→merciful
  • penny→penniless

y stays before -ing and -ish

  • baby→babyish
  • carry→carrying
  • copy→copying
  • hurry→hurrying
  • study→studying
  • worry→worrying

y stays after a vowel

  • annoy→annoying, annoyance
  • convey→conveyor
  • destroy→destroyer
  • employ→employer, employment
  • enjoy→enjoyment, enjoyable
  • journey→journeyed
  • joy→joyful
  • obey→obeyed
  • pay→payment, payable
  • play→player, playful
  • survey→surveyor

Words that break the rule

  • day→daily
  • lay→laid
  • pay→paid
  • say→said
  • shy→shyness, shyly (y stays)

After a consonant, y becomes i before an ending: lazy → laziness. It stays before -ing and -ish: study → studying. After a vowel, y stays: enjoy → enjoyment.

Adjectives ending in -ic add -ally. Adjectives ending in -al or -ful just add -ly, so you get a double l.

4. -our becomes -or

The u drops

  • glamour→glamorous
  • honour→honorary
  • labour→laborious
  • rigour→rigorous
  • vigour→vigorous

The u stays

  • colour→colourful
  • favour→favourable, favourite
  • honour→honourable
  • labour→labourer
  • neighbour→neighbourhood

In British spelling, a word ending in -our loses its u before -ous, -ious and -ary. Before other endings it keeps it.

5. -ise or -ize

  • advertise
  • advise
  • comprise
  • compromise
  • despise
  • devise
  • exercise
  • improvise
  • revise
  • supervise
  • surprise
  • televise

In British spelling both are right: recognise or recognize, organise or organize. These words are always -ise.

Watch out

  • -ful on its own has one l: beautiful, careful, helpful. The second l comes only with -ly: beautifully.
  • British spelling has one l in skilful; American English writes skillful.
  • dying comes from die; dyeing comes from dye, to colour something.
  • A few words take either spelling, and both are right: ageing or aging, likeable or likable.

Check yourself

Type the word made from the word in capitals, then press Check or Enter. The spelling has to be exact, as on the test.

  1. The scratch on my new bike is hardly (NOTICE).

  2. My brother and I had a big (ARGUE) about who would sit in the front.

  3. I am (TRUE) sorry for breaking your phone.

  4. After his best friend moved away, he struggled with (LONELY).

  5. Thank you for a very (ENJOY) evening.

  6. The story ends (TRAGIC): both heroes die.

  7. It was very (COURAGE) of you to tell the teacher the truth.

  8. The plants are (DIE) because nobody has watered them.

All eight right? Tick "I've learnt this" at the top, then practise in SmartEn.
