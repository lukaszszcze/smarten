Transformations and grammar

Inversion

When a sentence starts with a negative or limiting word such as never, hardly, not only or only when, the words after it go in question order: Never have I seen such a mess. In the online tests this is one of the hardest kinds of question for you. Of your 12 misses, four kept the usual word order, two left out one small word and four were left empty.

Common mistakes

These are the inversion questions you got wrong in the online tests. Where you left the gap empty, it says so.

I had only just sat down when the phone rang.HARDLY down when the phone rang.

Common mistakehardly sat / had hadly sat
RightHardly had I sat

Hardly comes first, then had, then I: Hardly had I sat down, like the question Had I sat down? Spell it h-a-r-d-l-y. The time is past perfect (had sat), because sitting down came before the phone rang.

Common mistakeThere's no way I'm (and once left empty)
RightIn no way am I

There's no way I'm is fine when you speak, but short forms like there's and I'm lose the point in this task. A gap at the start with WAY is a sign that the task wants In no way and the question order after it: am I.

Rightuntil I got home did I

Keep the tense of the plain sentence. I realised (past simple) becomes did I realise. had is only for the past perfect: I had realised becomes had I realised.

Not only at the start needs the question order. She baked becomes did she bake, with bake in its base form.

RightSo hot was the soup that I couldn't eat it.

The rule

Question order after a negative word

Put the negative or limiting word first. Then the words go as in a question: the helping verb, then the subject, then the main verb.

  • I have never seen such a mess. → Never have I seen such a mess. (Have I seen ...?)
  • She rarely complains. → Rarely does she complain. (Does she complain?)
  • I didn't know the truth. → Little did I know the truth. (Did I know ...?)

Which helping verb

Use the one the question would use. Keep the tense of the plain sentence.

The plain sentenceAfter the negative wordExample
present simpledo / does + subject + base formRarely do we eat out. Seldom does he smile.
past simpledid + subject + base formNot only did she bake the cake ... (not did she baked)
present perfecthave / has + subject + past participleNever have I seen such a mess.
past perfecthad + subject + past participleHardly had I sat down when the phone rang.
beam / is / are / was / were + subjectIn no way am I responsible. So hot was the soup that ...
will, can, must, shouldthe same verb + subject + base formNowhere will you find a better pizza. Under no circumstances must you open this door.

Phrases with two parts

  • Not only ... but (also): the question order only in the first part. Not only did she bake the cake, but she also made the decorations.
  • Hardly / Scarcely / Barely ... when and No sooner ... than: one thing happened just after another. The first part is past perfect, the second past simple. Hardly had I sat down when the phone rang. No sooner had I sat down than the phone rang.
  • So + adjective + be ... that: So hot was the soup that I couldn't eat it.
  • Such + be + noun ... that: Such was his surprise that he couldn't say a word. Use were with a plural noun.

Only and Not until: the question order comes later

After Only when, Only after, Only if, Only by and Not until, the whole first part comes first, in the usual order. The question order goes in the second part.

  • Only when I got home did I realise that I had lost my wallet.
  • Only by working hard can you pass the exam.
  • Not until I got home did I realise that I had lost my keys.
  • Only then and Only later have no first part, so the question order comes straight after them: Only then did I understand.

The same order in conditionals

A conditional can drop if and start with had, were or should in the same question order: Had I known, I would have come. Were I you, I would apologise. Should you need help, call me.

Watch out

  • Question order only when the negative word starts the sentence. In the middle of a sentence the order stays as usual: I have never seen such a mess. I had hardly sat down when the phone rang.
  • No question order when only or hardly belongs to the subject: Only Tom knew the answer. Hardly anyone came.
  • Hardly goes with when, No sooner with than. Never no sooner ... when.
  • After do, does and did the verb is in its base form: did I realise, not did I realised.
  • It was not until I got home that I realised means the same as Not until I got home did I realise, but it keeps the usual order. If the gap starts with It was, use this form.
  • No short forms in transformations: there's, I'm and didn't lose the point. Count the words too: up to six, the key word included. Under no circumstances must you is five.
  • Before you move on, read the whole sentence with your answer in it. A missing a, I or did is easy to hear that way.
