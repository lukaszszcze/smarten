Word formation and wisielec

Vowels you cannot hear clearly

In many English words a vowel is said so quickly that it sounds like a short "uh", whatever letter it is written with. In others, two vowel letters make one sound and you cannot hear which two. Saying these words aloud will not tell you how to spell them, so this page shows ways to work the spelling out, and lists the words to learn.

The rule

There is no single rule, but these six things help. The letters to watch are in green. Harder words are in the "Advanced" box under each list.

Write i before e (believe, chief) except after c (receive, ceiling). This works when you hear "ee". When you hear "ay" or "eye", it is usually ei (eight, height).

Watch out

  • mischievous has three parts, mis-chie-vous. There is no i before -ous.
  • stationary means not moving; stationery is paper and pens (e for envelope).
  • lose is the opposite of win; loose is the opposite of tight.
  • quite means fairly; quiet means not loud.
  • principal is the head of a school; a principle is a rule you believe in.
  • A compliment is something nice you say; to complement is to go well with something.
  • Use British spelling: colour, favourite, behaviour (American English writes color).
