Word formation and wisielec

Negative prefixes

un-, in-, im-, il-, ir- and dis- all turn a word into its opposite. Three of them are chosen by the first letter of the word, so they are easy. The choice between un-, in- and dis- has no rule that always works, so the lists below are the words to learn. And when the sentence means the opposite, do not leave the prefix off.

Common mistakes

Common mistakeunaccessible
Rightinaccessible

accessible takes in-.

Common mistakeundistinguishable
Rightindistinguishable

distinguishable takes in-.

Common mistakeundispensable
Rightindispensable

dispensable takes in-.

Common mistakeunseparated
Rightinseparable

separable takes in-; and the sentence needs -able: cannot be separated.

Common mistakedeniable
Rightundeniable

The sentence meant that nobody could deny it, so the prefix is needed.

The rule

The prefixes are in green. Harder words are in the "Advanced" box under each list.

1. il-, im- and ir-: chosen by the first letter

il- before l

  • legal→illegal
  • legible→illegible
  • legitimate→illegitimate
  • literate→illiterate
  • logical→illogical

im- before b, m and p

  • mature→immature
  • moral→immoral
  • mortal→immortal
  • patience→impatience
  • patient→impatient
  • perfect→imperfect
  • polite→impolite
  • possible→impossible
  • practical→impractical
Advanced: 10 more words
  • balance→imbalance
  • measurable→immeasurable
  • mobile→immobile
  • modest→immodest
  • partial→impartial (fair to everyone)
  • passable→impassable
  • personal→impersonal
  • probable→improbable
  • proper→improper
  • pure→impure

ir- before r

  • irresistible
  • rational→irrational
  • regular→irregular
  • relevant→irrelevant
  • repair→irreparable (the base word changes too)
  • replaceable→irreplaceable
  • respective→irrespective
  • responsible→irresponsible
  • reversible→irreversible

Before l write il-, before b, m and p write im-, before r write ir-. That is why these words have a double letter: il + legal = illegal.

Verbs: un- means doing the opposite

  • dress→undress

un- is the most common. It goes with ordinary English words, and nearly always with words ending in -ed and -ing: unexpected, unconvincing.

5. non- and mis-

non-

  • non-existent
  • non-fiction
  • non-smoker
  • non-stop
  • nonsense

mis-

  • behave→misbehave
  • interpret→misinterpret
  • judge→misjudge
  • lead→mislead
  • place→misplace
  • print→misprint
  • pronounce→mispronounce
  • spell→misspell
  • understand→misunderstand
  • use→misuse

non- simply means not: non-stop, non-fiction. mis- means wrongly, not "not": to misunderstand is to understand wrongly.

Watch out

  • inhabitable means a place can be lived in; uninhabitable means it cannot.
  • invaluable means extremely valuable, not without value.
  • infamous means famous for something bad.
  • disinterested means fair, not taking sides; uninterested means bored.
  • flammable and inflammable both mean that something burns easily.
  • unable and inability come from the same word, but take different prefixes. Learn each word on its own.
