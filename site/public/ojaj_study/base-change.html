Word formation and wisielec

When the base word changes

Most words are built by adding an ending to the word in capitals: care → careless, enjoy → enjoyment. Some words do not work like that. When the ending goes on, the base word changes inside: a letter drops out, the last letters change, an old ending comes off first, or the vowel changes. These words cannot be worked out by adding an ending, so learn them in pairs.

The rule

The lists group the words by what changes. The letters that go are crossed out and the letters that come in are green. Harder words are in the "Advanced" box under each list.

1. A letter drops out

The e before r

  • anger→angry
  • disaster→disastrous
  • enter→entrance
  • hinder→hindrance
  • hunger→hungry
  • minister→ministry
  • register→registration
  • remember→remembrance
Advanced: 5 more words
  • administer→administration
  • monster→monstrous
  • sober→sobriety
  • winter→wintry
  • wonder→wondrous

The i after a

  • exclaim→exclamation
  • explain→explanation
  • proclaim→proclamation
  • repair→irreparable
  • vain→vanity

The u of -ous

  • curious→curiosity
  • generous→generosity

Other letters

  • clear→clarity, clarify
  • pronounce→pronunciation

Say the new word aloud. A letter you no longer hear is usually not written either: in pro-nun-ci-a-tion you hear "nun", not "noun".

3. An old ending comes off first

-ous comes off

  • anxious→anxiety
  • famous→fame
  • notorious→notoriety
  • various→variety

-ion becomes -ious

  • ambition→ambitious
  • caution→cautious
  • infection→infectious
  • nutrition→nutritious
  • religion→religious
  • superstition→superstitious

-ble becomes -bility

  • able→ability
  • available→availability
  • flexible→flexibility
  • possible→possibility
  • probable→probability
  • reliable→reliability
  • responsible→responsibility
  • stable→stability
  • visible→visibility
Advanced: 5 more words
  • capable→capability
  • credible→credibility
  • humble→humility (humble loses its b too)
  • noble→nobility
  • sensible→sensibility

-ate comes off before -able

  • calculate→incalculable
  • irritate→irritable
  • negotiate→negotiable
  • separate→separable, inseparable

-or comes off before -ible

  • horror→horrible
  • terror→terrible

Other endings

  • commentate→commentary
  • politics→political, politician

Take the old ending off the base word, then put the new one on: anxi-ous, anxi-ety.

5. Countries and numbers

Countries

  • Britain→British
  • Denmark→Danish
  • England→English
  • Finland→Finnish
  • France→French
  • Greece→Greek
  • Ireland→Irish
  • Norway→Norwegian
  • Poland→Polish
  • Portugal→Portuguese
  • Scotland→Scottish
  • Spain→Spanish
  • Switzerland→Swiss
  • the Netherlands→Dutch
  • Wales→Welsh

Numbers

  • five→fifth
  • four→forty (but fourteen and fourth keep the u)
  • three→third
  • twelve→twelfth

Watch out

  • The vowel in speech sounds just like the one in speak, but it is spelt ee. Here saying the word aloud does not help: learn the spelling.
  • The ending -ment usually keeps the base word whole: announce → announcement, amaze → amazement. So it is pronunciation but announcement.
  • Not every f becomes v: roof → roofs, chief → chiefs, belief → beliefs.
  • deep → depth, but deepen keeps its ee.
  • advice and device are nouns; advise and devise are verbs.
  • deciduous is a real word, but it is about trees that lose their leaves in autumn. It has nothing to do with deciding. Someone who cannot decide is indecisive.

Check yourself

Type the word made from the word in capitals, then press Check or Enter. The spelling has to be exact, as on the test.

  1. Her (EXPLAIN) was so clear that everyone understood the problem at once.

  2. The first day of our holiday was (DISASTER): our flight was cancelled and our bags were lost.

  3. Everyone was amazed by her (GENEROUS): she gave half of her prize money to charity.

  4. My brother is very (PERSUADE): in the end he always gets what he wants.

  5. (ADMIT) to the museum is free for children under twelve.

  6. When the balloon burst, it sounded like a small (EXPLODE).

  7. Lifting that heavy box took all my (STRONG).

  8. The (WIDE) of the river here is about fifty metres.

All eight right? Tick "I've learnt this" at the top, then practise in SmartEn.
