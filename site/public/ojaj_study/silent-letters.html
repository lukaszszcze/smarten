Word formation and wisielec

Silent letters

Many English words have letters you do not hear: the k in knee, the b in doubt, the u in guess. They are left over from the way the words were said hundreds of years ago. Most of them follow a pattern, such as k before n or b after m, so learn them in groups.

Common mistakes

Common mistakegarantee
Rightguarantee

A silent u after g, as in guard and guess.

Common mistakeconcientious
Rightconscientious

It comes from conscience: con-sci-en-tious.

Common mistakegoverment
Rightgovernment

govern + ment: the n stays.

Common mistakeenviroment
Rightenvironment

environ + ment: the n stays.

Common mistakeFebuary
RightFebruary

Many people do not say the first r, but it is written.

The rule

A silent letter is often heard in a word from the same family. If you know the other word, you know the letter is there: sign → signature, resign → resignation, muscle → muscular, bomb → bombard, crumb → crumble, autumn → autumnal, condemn → condemnation, column → columnist, solemn → solemnity.

The silent letters are in green. Harder words are in the "Advanced" box under each list.

  • biscuit
  • build
  • built
  • circuit

After g, a silent u keeps the g hard, as in guess (without it, you would read "jess"). At the end of a word, -gue and -que are said like g and k.

Watch out

  • In cough, laugh, enough, rough and tough, gh is not silent: it sounds like f.
  • whose (whose bag is this?) and who's (who is) sound the same.
  • knew (from know) and new sound the same; so do knight and night, and write and right.
  • The silent letters stay when an ending is added: knowledgeable, doubtful, listening, designer.
