Transformations and grammar

Transformations: the checklist

In a key word transformation you complete the second sentence so that it means the same as the first, using the key word in capitals. You may write up to six words, the key word included. Knowing the grammar is only half of it: run the four checks below on every answer before you go on to the next question.

Common mistakes

Each of these answers has the right idea and still scores nothing.

When I saw my dad's new haircut, I couldn't stop laughing.HELP When I saw my dad's new haircut, I .

Common mistakewas helpless with laughter
Rightcould not help laughing

HELP has become helpless. The key word must stay exactly as it is printed.

Do you think I could borrow your pen for a moment?WONDER I borrow your pen for a moment.

Common mistakewas wondering if I could
Rightwonder if I could

"I was wondering if I could" is good English, but WONDER has become wondering.

It was Kasia's fault that the vase got broken.BLAME Kasia the broken vase.

Common mistakeis to blame for
Rightwas to blame for

The first sentence is about the past (was, got), so the answer must be in the past too.

The box was so heavy that I could not lift it.TOO The box was lift.

Common mistaketoo heavy to
Righttoo heavy for me to

The first sentence says that I could not lift it. "Too heavy to lift" leaves out who.

I cannot tolerate my brother's loud music any longer.PUT I cannot my brother's loud music any longer.

Common mistakeput op with
Rightput up with

One wrong letter, and the whole answer scores nothing.

Kasia is the only person who can help us.BUT Nobody us.

Common mistakebut Ksia can help
Rightbut Kasia can help

Names and words taken from the first sentence must be copied letter by letter.

The rule: four checks

  1. The key word, exactly as printed.

    Same letters, same ending: HELP stays help, WONDER stays wonder, KNOWN stays known. If the sentence you have in mind needs another form of the word, find a different way to say it that uses the word as it is.

  2. The same time as the first sentence.

    A sentence about the past gets an answer in the past. Look at the verbs in the first sentence and at words like yesterday, now, since and last year.

  3. The whole meaning.

    Every part of the first sentence must be in the new one: who does what, not, only, me, any more. Go through the first sentence piece by piece and find each piece in yours.

  4. Count, write in full, spell.

    At most six words, the key word included. No short forms: could not, never couldn't. Copy names and words from the sentence letter by letter. Last, read the whole new sentence from the first word to the full stop, to check that your words join the words after the gap.

Watch out

  • A sentence can be good English and still score nothing. If it changes the key word, the time or the meaning, it is wrong.
  • The key word must be in your answer. A perfect sentence without it scores nothing.
  • The words before and after the gap stay as they are. Your words have to fit between them.
  • Spelling counts in every word, not only in the key word.

Check yourself

Write your answer on paper first, run the four checks, then open the answer.

The last time I saw my cousin was in July.SEEN I my cousin since July.

have not seen. Not haven't: no short forms (check 4). Since goes with have seen, not with saw.

'You took my phone!' Ola said to Kuba.ACCUSED Ola her phone.

accused Kuba of taking. ACCUSED stays as it is printed (check 1), and Kuba has to be there, or we do not know who took the phone (check 3). After accuse someone of, use -ing.

The water was so cold that we could not swim in it.TOO The water was swim in.

too cold for us to. "Too cold to swim in" leaves out who could not swim (check 3).

Despite the rain, we went for a walk.ALTHOUGH raining, we went for a walk.

Although it was. The walk was in the past, so it was raining, not it is (check 2).

Please don't tell anyone about this.RATHER I tell anyone about this.

would rather you did not. After would rather you, the verb goes into the past: did not. Write it in full, not didn't (check 4).

Tom managed to finish the race in under an hour.SUCCEEDED Tom the race in under an hour.

succeeded in finishing. SUCCEEDED is printed in the past already, so it stays as it is (check 1). After succeed in, use -ing.

Kuba is the best goalkeeper in our team.BETTER Nobody in our team is Kuba.

a better goalkeeper than. "Better than Kuba" leaves out goalkeeper, and the first sentence is only about goalkeepers (check 3).
